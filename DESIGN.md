# Cool Head: design choices

The choices behind Cool Head's user journeys, workflows, UX, layout, build language, and more, each with the reason for it.

Color, type, icons, voice, and accessibility minimums for the app and its Chrome extension are summed up in [docs/BRAND.md](docs/BRAND.md): Cool Head blue is its own color, not the Mac's accent.

## Journeys

1. **"Why is my Mac hot?"** Click the menu bar icon. The panel opens on the answer: one card per cause, worst first. If all is calm, it says so in one line.
2. **It tells you first.** Something has been abnormal for a while (default 10 minutes): the menu bar badge changes (yellow triangle, or red octagon for a problem), and a notification says something needs a look, without naming it (macOS keeps notification text). No sound, at most one notification per cause, none for a card you chose to wait on.
3. **Act.** The card's one button, Explore fixes, shows how Cool Head knows and the options, each with its consequence. The options always come in one order: the recommended one first, as a filled button tagged Recommended; then options you can undo, most helpful first; then options that close or lose something, least loss first; then Share this case. Stopping asks once and names the process.
3a. **Not now.** Waiting isn't an option in the list: a clock in each card's corner hides it for 10 minutes, 30 minutes, or an hour (never "until it's done"); it comes back sooner if it gets worse. From the same clock, **Always trust** an app or job: it stays listed but calm, and is flagged anyway if it looks stuck, the Mac gets hot, or it has been busy nonstop for 8 hours. Trusted items are listed under Trusted, along the bottom of the panel, where + trusts an app and − stops trusting one.
3f. **Every time** (22 November 2026). Someone who knows an app always runs hot can say "always" without Cool Head acting without telling them. After Run it cooler works, the card asks "Slow <name> every time it runs?" with an Every time button, and says it can be stopped from Trusted, under Always cooler. A rule is kept by the same key as Trust (an app by its bundle identifier, so its helpers count too; a script or program by its location), on this Mac only, with the name to show. An app is slowed while it isn't the app in front, a few seconds after you switch away (so a download or a copy settles first), and is back to full speed the moment you switch to it, so the app you're using is never slowed; anything else is slowed whenever it runs, including each new launch, found on the 10-second sample. While a rule is slowing something, Managed by Cool Head shows two frosted boxes under their own headings: Just this once (until you speed it up or it quits) on top, since those need more attention, then Every time (until you stop it), one line per rule, with Speed up for now (until it quits) and Stop every time. Always cooler sits in the Trusted tool under Trusted, since both are standing choices about one app, with + for an app and − to remove one. A card about something with a rule says it already runs cooler every time and doesn't offer Run it cooler again. Trust and a rule are opposites, so adding one removes the other. Always, not only on battery. Only your own processes, as Run it cooler; what it slowed stays slowed if Cool Head quits, until it quits or comes to the front, and rules apply again when Cool Head next starts.
3d. **Battery and sync.** Battery Saver (first called Save Battery When Unplugged; on by default; from 16 November 2026 a switch on the Battery tool's page; since 19 November 2026 no switch at all: always on, shown as a card when it sees the problem, which also lets the Power page fit without scrolling, with Trust to quiet a job meant to run): on battery, a really hungry job (two cores for 10 minutes) or a long one (a core for 30 minutes), or below 50% battery any job using a core for 10 minutes (a script or tool, never an app), is flagged even when the Mac is cool, with Pause until you plug in (frozen, nothing lost); it carries on by itself when the Mac is plugged in, when Cool Head quits, and when it next starts. Stopping is still recommended only for a stalled job. Sync apps: Pause syncing for 1 hour, 3 hours, or until 8pm; Cool Head opens the app (it can't pause it itself) and reminds you to resume. Security, device management, and VPN software is never offered Stop, Run it cooler, or Quit.
3c. **Games come first.** A game (by its app category, or installed by Steam) is busy by nature: its card is calm, it is never offered Run it cooler (it would stutter), and while the Mac is warm it suggests lower graphics settings. While a game runs, other busy work is offered to run cooler first, to leave the game more power.
3b. **Warm means a cause.** While the Mac is warm, anything warming it that you can act on turns the badge yellow, and its card says it's adding to the heat; if nothing is working hard, a card says so and what usually explains it.
4. **Look closer.** Under the options, "Nerdy stuff (detailed info)" opens the evidence in place: why Cool Head recommends what it does, in plain words generated from the card's decision record (see docs/DIAGNOSIS.md), then the facts that led to the sentence, in plain lines, with anything that looks like a password hidden, in a small terminal, green on black, for a little flair, with its copy button in the title bar only once it's open. Nothing else to navigate.
5. **Unknown.** The card says it can't tell, and offers Look it up (a web search for the process name) and Share this case (an anonymised report, shown in full, that the person can open as a GitHub issue). Those, and Test my network's Start test (Apple's own network test, which talks to Apple's servers, and a request to Cloudflare's public trace page for where websites see the Mac), are the only ways anything leaves the Mac, and only when pressed.
6. **No looking back.** Cool Head is about now; it keeps no record of past causes, with one exception kept for one purpose: when memory was tight, so it can tell whether memory is what slows this Mac and how often (see No history below). It stays on this Mac, kept for that one purpose, and is disclosed rather than hidden. (A 24-hour chart and a "things today" list were tried and removed on 15 November 2026: it wasn't clear whether the chart showed temperature, speed, or load, and it gave nothing to act on, so it only added clutter.)

## Layout

The menu bar panel, 440 points wide, under the icon; its top and bottom stay put, and the middle grows to fit, up to 720 points or the screen's height less the top and bottom, then scrolls. With scroll bars that take room (Show scroll bars: Always, or a mouse), the panel grows to the right by the scroll bar's width while the middle scrolls, so nothing in it narrows or moves: the panel grows rather than fitting the scroll bar inside. One thing is open at a time in the middle, a card or Managed by Cool Head, so it rarely needs to scroll:

- Managed by Cool Head, at the top (called On hold until 19 November 2026, which could read as turned off rather than slowed or managed): open whenever nothing else is, since that saves a click and shows more at no cost: each time the panel opens, and when the last tool or Settings closes, but not when a card folds, so the cards don't move from under the pointer; folded, one frosted line with its count on a dark band, until clicked; open, anything Cool Head is running cooler, pausing, limiting, or has turned off, as frosted lines in one titled section, and sync apps waiting to be resumed. Each line's undo is a button on the line itself (Speed up, Resume, Remove limit, Turn on, Back it up; "I resumed it" for a sync app, which Cool Head can't resume), so the way to release each is plain to see, and under it what it is and since when, always shown, since the section is the only thing open. One Report a problem button under the lines covers all of them, and its form asks which one caused the trouble. Opening a tool folds the section, and opening the section closes the tool, as one tool closes another. A report's words and choices are kept, in memory only, through the panel closing or a tool opening, until it's sent or cancelled, so an accidental close or click loses nothing. The frost grows along every edge, most in the corners, with fine needles for texture and two wild ferns from the top, chosen from two rounds of renders; its numbers are in `FrostStyle` to tune.
- Cards: one short sentence each, with Explore fixes (worth watching) or Learn more (calm), and a clock in the corner to check back later. Open, a card shows its explanation, its recommended option, and then One click and Do it yourself, each folded to a line saying how many more options it holds; one opens at a time.
- At the very top, how the Mac is running, in a row tinted green, yellow, or red (there's no separate header, and it never says "Starting to melt", which would take the icon's picture too far): the menu bar icon's Melting Snowflake drawn larger, whole, melting, or in flames by the same level as the menu bar icon (anything slowing the Mac or needing action melts it; a serious problem sets it on fire); the status as its title ("1 to watch", "All calm", "Running hot · 1 looks stuck") over what's causing it in a few words; and, when Show usage and temperature is on in Settings (off to start), three round gauges, colored by level, in two titled groups with a line between, each named with its part's icon under it: Usage, Chip and Memory, then Temperature, one ring in the unit macOS's region settings use, showing the part furthest toward its own limit, the chip on a tie: the chip by macOS's four steps, the battery warm from 40°C and hot from 45°C (warmth while charging expected until 45°C), the drive warm from 70°C: the most worrying part, clearly labeled, not the highest number, which would nearly always be the chip's. When it shows the battery or drive, the sentence under the status names that part first. Hardware's chip temperature is the same live reading, every 3 seconds. Memory is macOS's own measure for memory pressure, never "Memory Used".
- Trends, a tool with the past week's energy use and why the Mac woke from sleep, was dropped on 19 November 2026, since the wake data served no clear purpose: its energy rows are on the Battery page, folded to "What used the most energy this week"; the wake history, which almost never led to anything to do, is left to `coolhead history`, and the sleep card covers wakes that cost battery.
- Along the bottom: the tools, each an icon with its name (names can be hidden), from the left This Mac, Power, Network, and Cleaner: three that show how the Mac is (Network's test acts, but what it gives is information), then the Cleaner, which changes things, with no line between them: the Cleaner is a different kind of tool, but the order already makes that clear: Network (Test my network), Power (a battery with a bolt on a laptop, a plug on a Mac with no battery; called Battery until 20 November 2026, a name that didn't fit a desktop), and This Mac (line drawn: a laptop when it has a battery, a desktop when it doesn't; called Hardware until 20 November 2026, and This Mac because it fits the bar), the checks; Cleaner (an open hard disk with sparkles, line drawn, the clearest sign of where it clears space, which is why This Mac is drawn as the Mac itself); and, at the right after a line, Report, Trusted, and Settings, since they're about Cool Head rather than the Mac (Trusted is a list, not a tool, so it sits between Report and Settings); Settings is the right-most. Each opens in the panel's middle, one at a time, and closes with the ✕ at its top right, the same in every tool; with one open and cards waiting, a pill under the top row's sentence says what they hold, "‹ 2 fixes recommended" (or "‹ 1 card to watch" when none recommends a fix), tinted with their level, and the top row's sentence no longer says "The cards below need a look", since that's the pill's whole point; the tools bar along the bottom takes the same wash of the level's color as the top row, so the two frame the middle; clicking the top of the panel also goes back to the cards. Test my network's results stay for 30 minutes after it or the panel closes, with the time and date of the test in bold beside Test again, so someone on the phone with their internet provider can move around the Mac and come back to them. A test that's started carries on when another tool opens or the panel closes, and its results wait, so the work isn't wasted; while it runs, dashes fly around the Network globe's equator in a single ring, like data going around the world, left to right across the front: orange around a globe in its usual color while Network is closed, and the other way round while it's open, gray dashes around the globe in the accent color, since the animation is part of the look (still with Reduce motion), and when it's done unseen the globe carries the result's mark from the view's top line, a green check when it's good, until it's opened. Only Stop, or Cool Head quitting, ends a test. No Quit or Update button: Quit is on the Settings page and in the right-click menu, and the version and Check Now are on the Settings page, so the bar has room for the tools' names. Power's page holds everything about the battery in one place, laid out like Test my network's result, so it's visual rather than a wall of text: a verdict in a few words, three figures (how much it holds, cycles against Apple's rating, temperature), and what it's doing now on one line; More battery life (Low Power Mode, which Cool Head can only open settings for), with no switch; then what used the most energy this week: the three largest without a click, "Show everything that used 5% or more this week (2 more)" for the rest, and every one with its share in Nerdy stuff; a Mac with no battery gets the page too, with a plug, "This Mac has no battery.", and the energy use, so it stays organized in one place rather than mixed in elsewhere. This Mac's page gives the model and serial number, with a copy button (shown only on the page, never in a report or the shared database), a verdict, then Storage: the startup disk's free space out of its size with a bar and Storage settings, which opens macOS's own breakdown (that breakdown comes from private parts of macOS, so Cool Head doesn't copy it; for now, free out of total and the button cover it), and Clean up, which opens the Cleaner, when under 20 GB is free, the memory card's own line; then each part with everything about it together: the drive's wear, writes, hours, temperature, and whether Time Machine backs it up; crash restarts, with when, what it means, and what to do; and port errors. Then memory and other parts, which macOS doesn't let apps test, with Apple Diagnostics' steps. No lines under the temperatures: the ring at the top names its part and shares the chip tile's reading, so an explanation there would only repeat them. The battery isn't on it: it has its own page. The Cleaner's page gives a verdict, then Quick clean, a list of what nothing uses and nothing has to rebuild, done with one button, since none of it is needed or has any effect when cleared, with Choose a folder… under it, which looks only in a folder you choose, anywhere, with Downloads chosen to start, and the folders inside it (not hidden ones, the inside of apps, or links to folders elsewhere; at most 100,000 files), for second downloads and Finder duplicates whose bytes match the original's (in Quick clean, not Deep clean, since it's quick and carries no risk), keeping the panel open while the chooser is up; then Deep clean, folded, with what rebuilds itself ticked (rebuilding can need the internet) and what Cool Head can't be sure of unticked and marked Caution, or with a padlock where it needs a password; then what Cool Head moved, to put back. Every count and item shows its size in B, KB, MB, or GB, and in each list items under 20 MB fold into one line laid out like the others, "3 smaller items, each under 20 MB" with their total and Show each one; in Deep clean that line has no tick of its own, so nothing marked Caution is ticked unseen. Then links to Privacy & Security, whose permission lists apps can't read, and to Apple's Storage settings. Every button asks first, and everything goes to the Trash. The Settings page, behind the gear, holds every setting so everything is in the panel: General (Notifications, Open at login, Check for updates automatically, with when it checks under it: a minute after Cool Head opens, then once a day, said as "When Cool Head opens, then daily." (every line under a setting is one short line, so the page isn't crowded with explanation), "Cool Head version" and the number, with the day it was released under it, and Check Now), then Look (the menu bar icon, both styles drawn at each level to pick by sight, each with one short line under its name, "Shapes and colors" and "Color-blind friendly"; Show tool names; and Show usage and temperature, off to start, for the gauges at the top, so anyone who doesn't know or care what they mean has less clutter), then About and Quit. The first-launch welcome offers the icon and Open at login, and points to the gear. Right-clicking the menu bar icon shows only Settings…, About, and Quit.

No tabs, no tables, no process list. Anyone who wants the raw list has Activity Monitor; Cool Head no longer links to it, since it isn't Cool Head's tool.

## Look

System font, system colors, generous spacing, one accent color for "needs a look". Light and dark follow macOS. Icons only where they carry meaning (heat, battery, a process's own app icon).

The logo and app icon are the menu bar's melting snowflake in white on Cool Head blue, its drops and puddle a paler white; the menu bar's Status badge style is one melting snowflake, with ice-blue drops and puddle, the same at every level, and only the badge changes. Both since 21 November 2026, replacing the smiling face and the plain snowflake, so the app icon and the menu bar are one family. The paler drops are more subtle on the app icon, which keeps their larger size, but they're harder to see in the menu bar, so the menu keeps its ice-blue ones for contrast. Details in docs/BRAND.md.

## Build language

**Swift with SwiftUI (recommended).** Native menu bar app, small (a few MB), lowest overhead, direct access to the system facts (process info, battery, thermal state). Every sentence is written by hand; Apple's on-device model was considered and dropped on 15 November 2026, since hand-written wording does as well without needing Apple Intelligence turned on (which takes up to 8 GB of storage) and without the risk of a rewording changing a fact. Builds on this Mac with the installed Command Line Tools. Weakest point: Mac-only, which is the point.

Alternatives considered: a web app in a native shell (Tauri or Electron) would make the design easier to iterate but would be 10 to 100 times larger, use more memory than the problems it reports, and still need native code for every system fact. Python would be quick to write but cannot make a proper menu bar app without extra layers.

## Other choices

- **No history:** Cool Head is not a record of the past. It samples every 10 seconds and keeps only what it needs in memory (recent readings, which cards have already notified); nothing is written to disk. The exceptions are the person's own choices, saved on this Mac as settings (no record of what happened): the apps and jobs they mark Always trust, reminders to resume a sync app they paused, jobs paused until the Mac is plugged in (so they are resumed even after a restart), and the Chrome memory limit an earlier Cool Head set, if any (so only its own is removed). One record of what happened is kept, for one purpose: spells of tight memory, 30 days, each with when it began and ended, how tight it got, how much was moved to disk, whether the Mac was slowed, and the kind of program using the most (never a name), so memory advice shows when memory really slows the Mac and the This Mac page can say how often it has. It stays on this Mac, kept for that purpose alone, and is disclosed here. Diagnostic logs exist only inside a case the user chooses to share, or where the law requires. The past week (from the bottom row) doesn't change this: it is macOS's own power log, which macOS keeps for about a week, read when the view is opened; Cool Head writes none of it to disk and keeps only the totals in memory until it quits, so Cool Head itself still stores no history.
- **Overhead target:** under 0.5% of the Mac on average (not shown in the panel, where a "Cool Head uses…" line added nothing).
- **Permissions:** none required. Full Disk Access is optional, only for reading other users' or system processes' files.
- **Distribution:** public on GitHub since 15 November 2026, ad-hoc signed (not notarized), with signed in-app updates (docs/UPDATES.md).
