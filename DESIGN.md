# Cool Head: design choices

Drafted 14 November 2026 for Phil's review, at his request: *"we also should possibly consider user journeys, workflows, UX, layout, build language and other choices"*. Each choice has a recommendation and the reason; Phil decides.

## Journeys

1. **"Why is my Mac hot?"** Click the menu bar icon. The panel opens on the answer: one card per cause, worst first. If all is calm, it says so in one line.
2. **It tells you first.** Something has been abnormal for a while (default 10 minutes): the menu bar badge changes (yellow triangle, or red octagon for a problem), and a notification says something needs a look, without naming it (macOS keeps notification text). No sound, at most one notification per cause, none for a card you chose to wait on.
3. **Act.** The card's one button, Explore fixes, shows how Cool Head knows and the options, each with its consequence. The options always come in one order (Phil, 15 November 2026): the recommended one first, as a filled button tagged Recommended; then options you can undo, most helpful first; then options that close or lose something, least loss first; then Share this case. Stopping asks once and names the process.
3a. **Not now.** Waiting isn't an option in the list: a clock in each card's corner hides it for 10 minutes, 30 minutes, or an hour (never "until it's done"); it comes back sooner if it gets worse. From the same clock, **Always trust** an app or job: it stays listed but calm, and is flagged anyway if it looks stuck, the Mac gets hot, or it has been busy nonstop for 8 hours. Trusted items are listed under Trusted, along the bottom of the panel, where + trusts an app and − stops trusting one.
3d. **Battery and sync** (Phil, 15 November 2026). Battery Saver (first called Save Battery When Unplugged; on by default; from 16 November 2026 a switch on the Battery tool's page; since 19 November 2026 no switch at all, always on, with Trust to quiet a job meant to run, Phil: "Maybe our battery saver option should be dropped from the power tool and instead display as a card for when we see the issue? That would also make the power tool fit on one page", then "Yes, drop the switch"): on battery, a really hungry job (two cores for 10 minutes) or a long one (a core for 30 minutes), or below 50% battery any job using a core for 10 minutes (a script or tool, never an app; Phil: *"only be aggressive to really hungry /extended processes or when battery is below 50%"*), is flagged even when the Mac is cool, with Pause until you plug in (frozen, nothing lost); it carries on by itself when the Mac is plugged in, when Cool Head quits, and when it next starts. Stopping is still recommended only for a stalled job. Sync apps: Pause syncing for 1 hour, 3 hours, or until 8pm; Cool Head opens the app (it can't pause it itself) and reminds you to resume. Security, device management, and VPN software is never offered Stop, Run it cooler, or Quit.
3c. **Games come first** (Phil, 15 November 2026). A game (by its app category, or installed by Steam) is busy by nature: its card is calm, it is never offered Run it cooler (it would stutter), and while the Mac is warm it suggests lower graphics settings. While a game runs, other busy work is offered to run cooler first, to leave the game more power.
3b. **Warm means a cause.** While the Mac is warm, anything warming it that you can act on turns the badge yellow, and its card says it's adding to the heat; if nothing is working hard, a card says so and what usually explains it.
4. **Look closer.** Under the options, "Nerdy stuff (detailed info)" opens the evidence in place: why Cool Head recommends what it does, in plain words generated from the card's decision record (see docs/DIAGNOSIS.md), then the facts that led to the sentence, in plain lines, with anything that looks like a password hidden, in a small terminal, green on black, with its copy button in the title bar once open (Phil, 19 November 2026: "the nerdy stuff section shouldn't show the copy icon until it's expanded"; "maybe we should add a fun Matrix or terminal-style background or design flair to the nerdy stuff section"). Nothing else to navigate.
5. **Unknown.** The card says it can't tell, and offers Look it up (a web search for the process name) and Share this case (an anonymised report, shown in full, that the person can open as a GitHub issue). Those, and Test my network's Start test (Apple's own network test, which talks to Apple's servers, and a request to Cloudflare's public trace page for where websites see the Mac), are the only ways anything leaves the Mac, and only when pressed.
6. **No looking back.** Cool Head is about now; it keeps no record of past causes. (A 24-hour chart and a "things today" list were tried and removed on 15 November 2026. Phil: *"I don't know what that chart/bar is at the bottom. Is it meant to be temperature? Speed? System resource load? It's not clear or actionable, which just adds confusing clutter."*)

## Layout

The menu bar panel, 440 points wide, under the icon; its top and bottom stay put, and the middle grows to fit, up to 720 points or the screen's height less the top and bottom, then scrolls. With scroll bars that take room (Show scroll bars: Always, or a mouse), the panel grows to the right by the scroll bar's width while the middle scrolls, so nothing in it narrows or moves (Phil, 18 November 2026: "Can we EXPAND the panel when we need a scroll bar instead of rendering it inside the panel?"). One thing is open at a time in the middle, a card or Managed by Cool Head, so it rarely needs to scroll (Phil, 18 November 2026: "only have one expanded thing at a time"):

- Managed by Cool Head, at the top (called On hold until Phil, 19 November 2026: "i don't like "on hold" as a user could interpret that as disabled, rather than throttled/managed"): open whenever nothing else is: each time the panel opens, and when the last tool or Settings closes, but not when a card folds, so the cards don't move from under the pointer (Phil, 19 November 2026: "When there's nothing open (i.e., fresh panel open, or after closing a tool or settings) let's auto-expand the "Managed by Cool Head" pane, because it's one less click for the user, more info, and "why not?""); folded, one frosted line with its count on a dark band, until clicked; open, anything Cool Head is running cooler, pausing, limiting, or has turned off, as frosted lines in one titled section, and sync apps waiting to be resumed. Each line's undo is a button on the line itself (Speed up, Resume, Remove limit, Turn on, Back it up; "I resumed it" for a sync app, which Cool Head can't resume) (Phil, 16 November 2026: "It needs to be clear to the user they they need to expand and then act, or we need to add a big old button right there to release it"), and under it what it is and since when, always shown, since the section is the only thing open (Phil, 19 November 2026: "As we only display one thing at a time, when the hold panel is open, we should expand them, no?"). One Report a problem button under the lines covers all of them, and its form asks which one caused the trouble. Opening a tool folds the section, and opening the section closes the tool, as one tool closes another (Phil, 19 November 2026: "Build the "managed by..." edits as suggested"). A report's words and choices are kept, in memory only, through the panel closing or a tool opening, until it's sent or cancelled (Phil, 19 November 2026: "can we protect text entered in a report in case of accidental closes or clicking a tool?"). The frost grows along every edge, most in the corners, with fine needles for texture and two wild ferns from the top, chosen by Phil from two rounds of renders ("I think version 4 of the frost is the best"); its numbers are in `FrostStyle` to tune.
- Cards: one short sentence each, with Explore fixes (worth watching) or Learn more (calm), and a clock in the corner to check back later. Open, a card shows its explanation, its recommended option, and then One click and Do it yourself, each folded to a line saying how many more options it holds; one opens at a time.
- At the very top, how the Mac is running, in a row tinted green, yellow, or red (there's no separate header: Phil, 17 November 2026, "The text shouldn't say "Starting to melt" - that's an abstraction taking the icon too far"): the menu bar icon's Melting Snowflake drawn larger, whole, melting, or in flames by the same level as the menu bar icon (anything slowing the Mac or needing action melts it; a serious problem sets it on fire); the status as its title ("1 to watch", "All calm", "Running hot · 1 looks stuck") over what's causing it in a few words; and three round gauges, colored by level, in two titled groups with a line between, each named with its part's icon under it (Phil, 19 November 2026: "Should we add a "usage" and "heat" title above the gauges with a vertical divider between the usage and heat?"; "if we are showing the icon for the temp gauge maybe we should for the usage as well?"): Usage, Chip and Memory, then Temperature, one ring in the unit macOS's region settings use, showing the part furthest toward its own limit, the chip on a tie: the chip by macOS's four steps, the battery warm from 40°C and hot from 45°C (warmth while charging expected until 45°C), the drive warm from 70°C (Phil, 19 November 2026: "most worrying part plus a clear label and/or icon"; not the highest number, which would nearly always be the chip's). When it shows the battery or drive, the sentence under the status names that part first. Hardware's chip temperature is the same live reading, every 3 seconds. Memory is macOS's own measure for memory pressure, never "Memory Used".
- Trends, a tool with the past week's energy use and why the Mac woke from sleep, was dropped (Phil, 19 November 2026: "Does the wakes data add value? Again, what purpose does it serve? Or should we drop this tool altogether?", then "Yes, drop Trends"): its energy rows are on the Battery page, folded to "What used the most energy this week" ("The energy usage can move into the power/battery tool"); the wake history, which almost never led to anything to do, is left to `coolhead history`, and the sleep card covers wakes that cost battery.
- Along the bottom: the tools, each an icon with its name (names can be hidden): Network (Test my network), Battery, and Hardware, the checks; Trusted; Cleaner (macOS's bubbles and sparkles; a drawn broom was hard to make out, Phil, 18 November 2026: "Option 5 for the cleaner icon, macOS bubbles and sparkles"); and, at the right after a line, Report and Settings, since they're about Cool Head rather than the Mac; Settings is the right-most (Phil, 17 November 2026). Each opens in the panel's middle, one at a time, and closes with the ✕ at its top right; with one open, clicking the top of the panel goes back to the cards (Phil, 19 November 2026: "The Trends tool also has a "Done" close option in the top right, while others have an "X""; "We can also make the top bar click to return to the main screen"). Test my network's results stay for 30 minutes after it or the panel closes, with the time and date of the test in bold beside Test again (Phil, 19 November 2026: "That way if they're on the phone with their ISP, for instance, they can toggle around their computer and refer back"). A test that's started carries on when another tool opens or the panel closes, and its results wait (Phil, 19 November 2026: "the network test should continue, complete, and store the results in the background. Otherwise the work is wasted"); while it runs out of sight, dashes fly around the Network globe's equator (still with Reduce motion), and when it's done unseen the globe carries the result's mark from the view's top line, a green check when it's good, until it's opened (Phil, 19 November 2026: "I meant the ring circumnavigating the globe like data packets flying around it as in a single ring along the equator"). Only Stop, or Cool Head quitting, ends a test. No Quit or Update button: Quit is on the Settings page and in the right-click menu, and the version and Check Now are on the Settings page, so the bar has room for the tools' names (Phil, 17 November 2026, choosing between renders: "Update in Settings"). Battery's page holds everything about the battery in one place: laid out like Test my network's result (Phil, 16 November 2026: "The battery tool isn't as visual as the network tool, it's a bit too much of a wall of text"): a verdict in a few words, three figures (how much it holds, cycles against Apple's rating, temperature), and what it's doing now on one line; More battery life (Low Power Mode, which Cool Head can only open settings for, and the week's energy use), with no switch; a Mac with no battery has no Battery tool (Phil, 16 November 2026: "that keeps it organized and tidy and succinct, rather than having mixed data in mixed places"). Hardware's page (a drive icon, since most of it is the drive) gives a verdict, then each part with everything about it together (Phil, 16 November 2026: "we should keep related info together"): the drive's wear, writes, hours, temperature, and whether Time Machine backs it up; crash restarts, with when, what it means, and what to do; and port errors. Then memory and other parts, which macOS doesn't let apps test, with Apple Diagnostics' steps. The battery isn't on it: it has its own page. The Cleaner's page (Phil, 18 November 2026: "Can we add a broom "cleaner" tool"; then "The first section one-button should be stuff that definitely isn't needed and has no impact, trash. Then the second section, something like "Deep Clean", where you can select things should auto-check the stuff that rebuilds and have the "check these first" as options to check.") gives a verdict, then Quick clean, a list of what nothing uses and nothing has to rebuild, done with one button, with Choose a folder… under it, which looks only in a folder you choose, anywhere, with Downloads chosen to start, and the folders inside it (not hidden ones, the inside of apps, or links to folders elsewhere; at most 100,000 files; Phil, 19 November 2026: "Please add searching in child folders"), for second downloads and Finder duplicates whose bytes match the original's (Phil, 18 November 2026: "Check for duplicate files under deep clean should be under quick clean, as it's a quick zero-risk choice"; 19 November 2026: "word it more openly to check anywhere for duplicates, and then just default to the downloads folder"), keeping the panel open while the chooser is up; then Deep clean, folded, with what rebuilds itself ticked (rebuilding can need the internet) and what Cool Head can't be sure of unticked and marked Caution, or with a padlock where it needs a password; then what Cool Head moved, to put back. Every count and item shows its size in B, KB, MB, or GB (Phil, 18 November 2026: "when we show the number of files moved, we also need to show the file size (B/KB/MB/GB)"), and in each list items under 20 MB fold into one line laid out like the others, "3 smaller items, each under 20 MB" with their total and Show each one (Phil, 18 November 2026: "The smaller items folded together should be a single line item that matches the rest of the list unless expanded"); in Deep clean that line has no tick of its own, so nothing marked Caution is ticked unseen. Then links to Privacy & Security, whose permission lists apps can't read, and to Apple's Storage settings. Every button asks first, and everything goes to the Trash. The Settings page, behind the gear, holds every setting so everything is in the panel (Phil, 17 November 2026): General (Notifications, Open at login, Check for updates automatically, "Cool Head version" and the number, with the day it was released under it, and Check Now), then Look (the menu bar icon, both styles drawn at each level to pick by sight, and Show tool names), then About and Quit. The first-launch welcome offers the icon and Open at login, and points to the gear. Right-clicking the menu bar icon shows only Settings…, About and Quit (Phil, 17 November 2026: "or we put back the settings icon in the tool bar and limit right click to quit").

No tabs, no tables, no process list. Anyone who wants the raw list has Activity Monitor; Cool Head no longer links to it, since it isn't Cool Head's tool (Phil, 16 November 2026).

## Look

System font, system colors, generous spacing, one accent color for "needs a look". Light and dark follow macOS. Icons only where they carry meaning (heat, battery, a process's own app icon).

## Build language

**Swift with SwiftUI (recommended).** Native menu bar app, small (a few MB), lowest overhead, direct access to the system facts (process info, battery, thermal state). Every sentence is written by hand; Apple's on-device model was considered and dropped on 15 November 2026, since hand-written wording does as well without needing Apple Intelligence turned on (which takes up to 8 GB of storage) and without the risk of a rewording changing a fact. Builds on this Mac with the installed Command Line Tools. Weakest point: Mac-only, which is the point.

Alternatives considered: a web app in a native shell (Tauri or Electron) would make the design easier to iterate but would be 10 to 100 times larger, use more memory than the problems it reports, and still need native code for every system fact. Python would be quick to write but cannot make a proper menu bar app without extra layers.

## Other choices

- **No history (Phil, 15 November 2026):** Cool Head is not a record of the past. It samples every 10 seconds and keeps only what it needs in memory (recent readings, which cards have already notified); nothing is written to disk. The exceptions are the person's own choices, saved on this Mac as settings (no record of what happened): the apps and jobs they mark Always trust, reminders to resume a sync app they paused, jobs paused until the Mac is plugged in (so they are resumed even after a restart), and the Chrome memory limit Cool Head set, if any (so undo removes only its own). Diagnostic logs exist only inside a case the user chooses to share, or where the law requires. The past week (from the bottom row) doesn't change this: it is macOS's own power log, which macOS keeps for about a week, read when the view is opened; Cool Head writes none of it to disk and keeps only the totals in memory until it quits (Phil, 16 November 2026: "If it's stored by macOS we don't need to revise our rule as we're not storing history.").
- **Overhead target:** under 0.5% of the Mac on average (not shown in the panel; Phil, 15 November 2026: the "Cool Head uses…" line was unnecessary).
- **Permissions:** none required. Full Disk Access is optional, only for reading other users' or system processes' files.
- **Distribution:** public on GitHub since 15 November 2026, ad-hoc signed (not notarized), with signed in-app updates (docs/UPDATES.md).
