# Cool Head: design choices

Drafted 14 November 2026 for Phil's review, at his request: *"we also should possibly consider user journeys, workflows, UX, layout, build language and other choices"*. Each choice has a recommendation and the reason; Phil decides.

## Journeys

1. **"Why is my Mac hot?"** Click the menu bar icon. The panel opens on the answer: one card per cause, worst first. If all is calm, it says so in one line.
2. **It tells you first.** Something has been abnormal for a while (default 10 minutes): the menu bar badge changes (yellow triangle, or red octagon for a problem), and a notification says something needs a look, without naming it (macOS keeps notification text). No sound, at most one notification per cause, none for a card you chose to wait on.
3. **Act.** The card's one button, Explore fixes, shows how Cool Head knows and the options, each with its consequence. The options always come in one order (Phil, 15 November 2026): the recommended one first, as a filled button tagged Recommended; then options you can undo, most helpful first; then options that close or lose something, least loss first; then Share this case. Stopping asks once and names the process.
3a. **Not now.** Waiting isn't an option in the list: a clock in each card's corner hides it for 10 minutes, 30 minutes or an hour (never "until it's done"); it comes back sooner if it gets worse. From the same clock, **Always trust** an app or job: it stays listed but calm, and is flagged anyway if it looks stuck, the Mac gets hot, or it has been busy nonstop for 8 hours. Trusted items are listed under Trusted, along the bottom of the panel, where + trusts an app and − stops trusting one.
3d. **Battery and sync** (Phil, 15 November 2026). Battery Saver (first called Save Battery When Unplugged; on by default; since 16 November 2026 a switch on the Battery tool's page, because it's switched as your situation changes): on battery, a really hungry job (two cores for 10 minutes) or a long one (a core for 30 minutes), or below 50% battery any job using a core for 10 minutes (a script or tool, never an app; Phil: *"only be aggressive to really hungry /extended processes or when battery is below 50%"*), is flagged even when the Mac is cool, with Pause until you plug in (frozen, nothing lost); it carries on by itself when the Mac is plugged in, when Cool Head quits, and when it next starts. Stopping is still recommended only for a stalled job. Sync apps: Pause syncing for 1 hour, 3 hours or until 8pm; Cool Head opens the app (it can't pause it itself) and reminds you to resume. Security, device management and VPN software is never offered Stop, Run it cooler or Quit.
3c. **Games come first** (Phil, 15 November 2026). A game (by its app category, or installed by Steam) is busy by nature: its card is calm, it is never offered Run it cooler (it would stutter), and while the Mac is warm it suggests lower graphics settings. While a game runs, other busy work is offered to run cooler first, to leave the game more power.
3b. **Warm means a cause.** While the Mac is warm, anything warming it that you can act on turns the badge yellow, and its card says it's adding to the heat; if nothing is working hard, a card says so and what usually explains it.
4. **Look closer.** Under the options, "The facts" opens the evidence in place: the facts that led to the sentence, in plain lines, with anything that looks like a password hidden. Nothing else to navigate.
5. **Unknown.** The card says it can't tell, and offers Look it up (a web search for the process name) and Share this case (an anonymised report, shown in full, that the person can open as a GitHub issue). Those, and Test my network's Start test (Apple's own network test, which talks to Apple's servers), are the only ways anything leaves the Mac, and only when pressed.
6. **No looking back.** Cool Head is about now; it keeps no record of past causes. (A 24-hour chart and a "things today" list were tried and removed on 15 November 2026. Phil: *"I don't know what that chart/bar is at the bottom. Is it meant to be temperature? Speed? System resource load? It's not clear or actionable, which just adds confusing clutter."*)

## Layout

The menu bar panel, 412 points wide, under the icon; its top and bottom stay put and the middle scrolls when it's long:

- Status line: "Running warm · 1 to watch" or "All calm".
- Pinned at the top: anything running cooler or paused (frosted), and sync apps waiting to be resumed. Each line's undo is a button on the line itself (Speed up, Resume, Remove limit, Turn on, Back it up; "I resumed it" for a sync app, which Cool Head can't resume), and a chevron alone opens what it is (Phil, 16 November 2026: "It needs to be clear to the user they they need to expand and then act, or we need to add a big old button right there to release it"). The frost grows along every edge, most in the corners, with fine needles for texture and two wild ferns from the top, chosen by Phil from two rounds of renders ("I think version 4 of the frost is the best"); its numbers are in `FrostStyle` to tune.
- Cards: one short sentence each, with Explore fixes (worth watching) or Learn more (calm), and a clock in the corner to check back later.
- At the top, under the header, how the Mac is running (Phil, 16 November 2026: "Maybe the temperature panes from the hardware screen renders should be persistent in the main pane instead?"): the menu bar icon's Melting Snowflake drawn larger, whole, melting or in flames by macOS's thermal state, with a few words, and two gauges, Chip and Memory, each a percentage over a bar coloured by level; no tooltips, since the colour, the words and the cards say whether it matters. Memory is macOS's own measure for memory pressure, never "Memory Used".
- Along the bottom: the tools, each an icon with its name (names can be hidden): Network (Test my network), Battery and Hardware, the checks; Trends (the past week's energy use, and why it woke from sleep, from macOS's power log); Trusted; and Report at the right, since it's about Cool Head rather than the Mac. Each opens in the panel's middle, one at a time. Quit is in the right-click menu only (Phil, 16 November 2026). Battery's page holds everything about the battery in one place: laid out like Test my network's result (Phil, 16 November 2026: "The battery tool isn't as visual as the network tool, it's a bit too much of a wall of text"): a verdict in a few words, three figures (how much it holds, cycles against Apple's rating, temperature), and what it's doing now on one line; More battery life (Low Power Mode, which Cool Head can only open settings for, and the week's energy use), and Battery Saver's switch last; a Mac with no battery has no Battery tool (Phil, 16 November 2026: "that keeps it organized and tidy and succinct, rather than having mixed data in mixed places"). Hardware's page (a drive icon, since most of it is the drive) gives a verdict, then each part with everything about it together (Phil, 16 November 2026: "we should keep related info together"): the drive's wear, writes, hours, temperature and whether Time Machine backs it up; crash restarts, with when, what it means and what to do; and port errors. Then memory and other parts, which macOS doesn't let apps test, with Apple Diagnostics' steps. The battery isn't on it: it has its own page. Tools go along the bottom and settings in the right-click menu (Phil, 16 November 2026). The name and version ("Cool Head v0.4.5") sit small at the top right; clicking them checks for updates. Right-clicking the menu bar icon shows Check for Updates… and Check for Updates Automatically, then a Settings submenu of toggles (Notifications and Open at Login; then how it looks: Hide Tool Names and Melting Snowflake Icon), then About and Quit, every item aligned (Phil, 16 November 2026).

No tabs, no tables, no process list. Anyone who wants the raw list has Activity Monitor; Cool Head no longer links to it, since it isn't Cool Head's tool (Phil, 16 November 2026).

## Look

System font, system colors, generous spacing, one accent color for "needs a look". Light and dark follow macOS. Icons only where they carry meaning (heat, battery, a process's own app icon).

## Build language

**Swift with SwiftUI (recommended).** Native menu bar app, small (a few MB), lowest overhead, direct access to the system facts (process info, battery, thermal state). Every sentence is written by hand; Apple's on-device model was considered and dropped on 15 November 2026, since hand-written wording does as well without needing Apple Intelligence turned on (which takes up to 8 GB of storage) and without the risk of a rewording changing a fact. Builds on this Mac with the installed Command Line Tools. Weakest point: Mac-only, which is the point.

Alternatives considered: a web app in a native shell (Tauri or Electron) would make the design easier to iterate but would be 10 to 100 times larger, use more memory than the problems it reports, and still need native code for every system fact. Python would be quick to write but cannot make a proper menu bar app without extra layers.

## Other choices

- **No history (Phil, 15 November 2026):** Cool Head is not a record of the past. It samples every 10 seconds and keeps only what it needs in memory (recent readings, which cards have already notified); nothing is written to disk. The exceptions are the person's own choices, saved on this Mac as settings (no record of what happened): the apps and jobs they mark Always trust, reminders to resume a sync app they paused, jobs paused until the Mac is plugged in (so they are resumed even after a restart), and the Chrome memory limit Cool Head set, if any (so undo removes only its own). Diagnostic logs exist only inside a case the user chooses to share, or where the law requires. The past week (from the bottom row) doesn't change this: it is macOS's own power log, which macOS keeps for about a week, read when the view is opened; Cool Head writes none of it to disk and keeps only the totals in memory until it quits (Phil, 16 November 2026: "If it's stored by macOS we don't need to revise our rule as we're not storing history.").
- **Overhead target:** under 0.5% of the Mac on average (not shown in the panel; Phil, 15 November 2026: the "Cool Head uses…" line was unnecessary).
- **Permissions:** none required. Full Disk Access is optional, only for reading other users' or system processes' files.
- **Distribution:** public on GitHub since 15 November 2026, ad-hoc signed (not notarized), with signed in-app updates (docs/UPDATES.md).
