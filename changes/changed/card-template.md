- Every card now follows one template, in simple English that's easier to read if English isn't your first language. The headline says what it's about, how it's doing, and what Cool Head thinks, from a short fixed list ("A Google Chrome tab is still working hard after 1 hour. This is more than usual.", "Model made its last progress at 1:39pm. It is probably stuck."); how hard something works, in cores, is in Nerdy stuff. A memory card says what's using it rather than that the Mac is low ("Apps and programs are using almost all of your Mac's memory. Google Chrome uses the most, 5.1 GB."). Each option says what it does, then what you lose, then whether you can undo it ("This slows it, so it makes less heat. You can undo this."), and explanations are 3 sentences at most. Cards no longer use contractions, idioms, or "we", and use one word for each idea; Look it up is now Search the web, and Share this case is now Send this case. When a card mentions memory or storage, one line under the cards says what each is ("Memory is where apps and programs work. Storage is where your files are kept.").
- The memory card says each figure once. Quit for the app using the most no longer repeats the size the headline gives, and an option that frees something says how much in its title ("Quit Slack instead to free 1.1 GB", "Unload llama3.2:3b to free 3.2 GB", "Delete Xcode's build data to free 14 GB of storage"), unless the headline already says it. An app that's probably leaking says what it grew from and to ("Pages grew from 1.1 GB to 8.5 GB in the last 3 hours. It probably leaks memory."), and its option says about how much restarting it gives back.
